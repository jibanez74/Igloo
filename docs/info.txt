`expo-video` does **not define its own codec support** the way VLC or FFmpeg would. It uses the native platform player:

* **Android / Android TV / Fire TV:** ExoPlayer / Android Media3, backed mostly by Android platform decoders.
* **iOS / tvOS / Apple TV:** AVPlayer / AVFoundation.
* **Web:** browser media support.

Expo’s docs list `expo-video` as supporting Android, iOS, tvOS, and Web, and its source type includes progressive, HLS, DASH, and SmoothStreaming modes. ([Expo Documentation][1])

## Practical codec support

### Video codecs likely to work

For a media-center app, the realistic safe set is:

| Codec            |                                                                  Android TV / Fire TV |                         Apple TV / tvOS | Notes                                                     |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------: | --------------------------------------: | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **H.264 / AVC**  |                                                                                   Yes |                                     Yes | Safest universal choice.                                  |
| **H.265 / HEVC** | Usually yes on modern Android TV; Android TV baseline support is stronger than phones |                  Yes on modern Apple TV | Device/profile/level dependent.                           |
| **VP8**          |                                                                        Yes on Android |              Not a good Apple TV target | Mostly useful for WebM/Android.                           |
| **VP9**          |                                                                  Common on Android TV |              Not a good Apple TV target | Good for Android/YouTube-like pipelines, not Apple-first. |
| **AV1**          |                             Android support depends heavily on device/Android version | Newer Apple devices only; not universal | Not safe as your only encode.                             |

Android’s platform docs list H.264, H.265/HEVC, VP8, VP9, and AV1 support, but also warn that support varies by device and form factor. ([Android Developers][2]) ExoPlayer also says sample-format support depends on Android platform decoders by default. ([Android Developers][3])

### Audio codecs likely to work

| Codec                           |                                                      Android TV / Fire TV |                           Apple TV / tvOS | Notes                                         |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------: | ----------------------------------------: | --------------------------------------------- |
| **AAC-LC**                      |                                                                       Yes |                                       Yes | Safest universal audio codec.                 |
| **HE-AAC**                      |                                                                       Yes |                                       Yes | Good for low bitrate audio.                   |
| **MP3**                         |                                                                       Yes |                               Usually yes | Fine, but not ideal for video files.          |
| **Opus**                        |                                                               Android yes | Not reliable for Apple TV native playback | Avoid for universal TV playback.              |
| **Vorbis**                      |                                                               Android yes |                 Not reliable for Apple TV | Avoid for universal TV playback.              |
| **FLAC**                        | Android support exists but limited; multichannel is not a safe assumption |       Apple supports FLAC in HLS contexts | Not ideal as primary TV-app audio.            |
| **AC-3 / Dolby Digital**        |                                                          Device dependent |     Supported in Apple HLS authoring spec | Useful for 5.1 compatibility.                 |
| **E-AC-3 / Dolby Digital Plus** |                                                          Device dependent |     Supported in Apple HLS authoring spec | Common streaming 5.1 / Atmos carrier.         |
| **DTS / DTS-HD**                |                            Not safe without custom/native decoder support |                                  Not safe | Do not assume `expo-video` will play it.      |
| **TrueHD**                      |                                                                  Not safe |                                  Not safe | Do not assume support. Transcode/remux audio. |

Android’s standard audio table includes AAC, HE-AAC, xHE-AAC, MP3, FLAC, Opus, Vorbis, and PCM/WAV, but support details vary by container and device. ([Android Developers][2]) Apple’s HLS authoring spec includes AAC-LC, HE-AAC, Apple Lossless, FLAC, Dolby Digital / AC-3, and Dolby Digital Plus / E-AC-3 among supported multichannel audio codecs. ([Apple Developer][4])

## Important for your Igloo/media-center use case

For **direct play**, `expo-video` is not equivalent to VLC, Kodi, or a Jellyfin client with FFmpeg-backed fallback. It will not magically decode every MKV/Blu-ray-style file.

The safest universal output target is:

```text
Container/stream: HLS or MP4
Video: H.264
Audio: AAC-LC stereo
Optional surround track: AC-3 or E-AC-3 5.1
Subtitles: WebVTT for HLS
```

For higher quality TV playback:

```text
Container/stream: HLS
Video: H.264 + HEVC variants
Audio: AAC stereo + E-AC-3 5.1 when supported
```

For Android TV only, you can be more flexible with MKV, WebM, VP9, Opus, etc., because ExoPlayer supports many progressive containers including MP4, M4A, WebM, Matroska/MKV, MP3, Ogg, WAV, MPEG-TS, FLV, ADTS, FLAC, and AMR. ([Android Developers][3]) But for a cross-platform TV app, you should not build your direct-play strategy around MKV + DTS/TrueHD.

## Bottom line

For your project, assume:

```text
Direct play safe:
H.264 + AAC in MP4/HLS

Direct play likely on many TVs:
HEVC + AAC/E-AC-3 in MP4/HLS

Needs fallback/transcoding:
DTS, DTS-HD, TrueHD, many MKV files, obscure subtitle formats, unsupported HEVC profiles, some HDR/Dolby Vision cases
```

So `expo-video` can work for a TV media app, but your backend still needs the same kind of compatibility decision layer you were already designing: detect the client, inspect video/audio/subtitle tracks, then direct play, remux, or transcode depending on what the device can actually handle.
